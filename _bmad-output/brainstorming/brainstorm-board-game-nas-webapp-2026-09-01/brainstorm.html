Technique 01 · Job to Be Done

What's the real job?

Strip away features and solutions. What are people actually hiring this app to do?

When… we sit down for game night and someone pulls out a box no one's touched in months
I want… to get everyone up to speed and playing as fast as possible — no rule arguments, no hunting for the manual
So that… the evening is about laughing and playing together, not explaining and re-explaining
⏱️
Core Pain

The Rules Bottleneck

Rule explanation and setup refreshers eat the most time before a game starts. Everything else is fine — this is the one thing worth solving first.

📲
The Flow

One QR, Everyone In

Host selects the game on their phone — a QR code appears. Friends scan it and land directly in the right game room. No install, no account, no friction.

📱
Surface Reality

Phone First, Shared Screen Maybe

Everyone's at the same table. Primary surface is the personal phone. TV via Chromecast is possible but uncertain — don't build for it as baseline.

🃏
Mid-Game Need

Private Reference Without Interrupting

Players need to quietly check a rule or a card effect on their own phone mid-game — without asking out loud and giving away their hand.

Technique 02 · Persona Journey

Who's at the table?

Four familiar faces at every game night. The app has to work for all of them — or it works for none of them.

🙄
The Skeptic
Doesn't want to be managed

Plays games to have fun with friends, not to interact with software. Any friction — a loading spinner, an unexpected prompt — is a reason to not use it next time.

"Just be there when I need you. Invisible when I don't."
📖
The Rules Lawyer
Needs to get it exactly right

Needs layered rules — the simple version first, but must be able to drill all the way down to the full official wording when a dispute arises. Summary ≠ truth.

"The cheat sheet is fine — but show me the actual ruling."
✨
The First-Timer
Already learning two things

Already learning a new game — can't also wrestle with a complicated app. The app itself must be dead simple, or it becomes another thing that raises the barrier.

"I just want to know what I can do on my turn."
😤
The Sore Loser
Needs a neutral authority

"Just Google it" starts arguments — everybody finds a different forum post. An in-context AI arbiter that knows the game and explains rulings neutrally defuses tension.

"Let the app decide. It doesn't have a stake in this."
Technique 03 · Sci-Fi Artifact From the Future

What does it become?

Fast-forward five years. The app has been running. What has accumulated? What does it hold that it couldn't have held at launch?

📜

The Friendship Chronicle

Session history isn't a log — it's a record of your group's gaming life together. Every game played, every comeback, every lopsided victory, growing quietly across years.

📸

Sessions With Faces

Photos attached to sessions. Scroll back and see who was there, what was on the table, what the room looked like on a random Thursday two Decembers ago.

🕯️

"On This Day" Memories

The app surfaces past session memories on their anniversary — a quiet nudge: "Two years ago tonight you played Wingspan and someone won by a single point."

🏠
Core Design Principle — Yours First

The app is Baptiste's personal tool first. Sessions are shared only when others are willing — never pushed. Participation is always opt-in, never assumed. The app serves the host, and through the host, the group.

Technique 04 · SCAMPER Method

Remix, adapt, reimagine.

Substitute, Combine, Adapt, Modify, Put to another use, Eliminate, Rearrange. Eight ideas from pressing on the shape of the thing.

S

Don't Replace the PDF

The official rulebook stays as the authoritative source — it can't be substituted. AI content lives alongside it, enriching it, but never replacing it.

C

Cheat Sheet + Rules = One Thing

Tapping a cheat sheet item deep-links directly into the relevant rule section. The summary becomes a navigation layer over the full rulebook.

A

Reading App UX for Rules

Zoom, quick page flip, jump to sections — patterns from Kindle and reading apps, adapted for rulebooks and quick-starts instead of novels.

A

Cook Mode for Quick-Start

Screen stays awake. Tap to advance through steps. No lock screen mid-explanation. Borrowed from recipe apps — your hands are occupied, your eyes are on the table.

M

Quality Voting on AI Content

AI-generated cheat sheets carry uncertainty. Bad ratings trigger regeneration. The content improves through use — trust is earned, not assumed.

M

Adaptive Quick-Start

Quick-start checks each player's history with the game. Experienced players have the basics auto-skipped. The room's collective knowledge shapes the explanation.

P

History as a Discovery Engine

The session log isn't just a record — it powers game recommendations. Not limited to the owned shelf; suggests new games based on what the group actually enjoyed.

R

Game Addition as a Pipeline

Adding a game triggers a flow: fetch rulebook → AI learns it → generates cheat sheet + quick-start → back-and-forth review → approved and published to shelf.

Synthesis · Key Insights

What did we learn?

Two insights that reframe everything above — and make the app more coherent than the sum of its features.

Insight 01 · The Learning Loop

The app gets smarter through use. Session history improves recommendations. Quality votes improve AI content. These aren't two separate feedback systems — they're the same loop. The more it's used, the better it becomes, automatically.

Insight 02 · The Personal Window

Private phone view, interactive scorecards, and AI card lookup look like three separate features. They're not. They're one unified concept: a personal window that belongs to each player, giving them private context without interrupting the shared table.

The Learning Loop — visualised

Both history improvement and content improvement feed the same cycle — use and quality are not separate concerns.

🪟
The Personal Window — one feature, not three

Private phone view + interactive scorecards + AI card lookup share the same design question: what does this player need right now that only they should see? Solve it once and all three come along for free.
